How the three models bet

Three models look at the same Polymarket barrier markets every 15 minutes and decide which bets to buy. They share one signal, the option-implied probability of a touch, and differ only in how they build a book from it. This page explains each rule and lets you try it on your own numbers. The calculators run the same arithmetic as the Python, checked to within 1e-7 on a sample book.

1. A bet

Every market is a barrier on a stock before a deadline, for example "Will EWY reach $194 by Friday?" (a hit_up market) or "Will TSLA fall to $367.50?" (a hit_down market). Each market has two sides. A share pays $1 if its side wins and $0 if it loses.

How a bet is judged models/bets.py · Bet
  • cost = best ask + Polymarket's fee, 0.04 × min(price, 1 − price) per share.
  • value = the model's win probability, minus a 2¢ risk premium on a YES (YES touches lost money at every bar in September).
  • EV = value − cost per share, and ROI = EV ÷ cost.
  • Bar: a bet clears if EV ≥ 3¢ and ROI ≥ 8%. Above a 90¢ ask the bar is EV ≥ 0 and ROI ≥ 2%, because 3¢ on a 95¢ NO is never on offer.
  • Limit: the harness buys deeper levels of the book only while each still clears the bar, so every share bought is a good one. The limit is min(value − EV bar, value ÷ (1 + ROI bar)).

2. One cycle

Every 15 minutes the harness replays one snapshot of the books. A model never sees an order book, an API or a ledger. It gets a list of bets on offer (every bet with EV ≥ 0, best ROI first) and its holdings, and it calls take_bet or refuse for each offer.

The model chooses which bets and up to how much. The harness then applies the rules every model shares:

  • Per-market cap: $150 on a YES market, $500 on a NO market.
  • Depth: at most half of each price level, and only levels below the bet's limit.
  • One entry per market: a market already held is never bought again, on either side.
  • Minimum: fills under $1 (Polymarket's minimum order) are dropped.
  • Cash: the $10,000 bankroll. Per-ticker and per-direction caps apply where a model sets them.

A bet can ask the market bet.quote(usd, limit), which returns what that much money would actually buy right now after depth and caps. The hedged models use it to size before they commit.

3. edge_taker

models/edge_taker.py · 12 lines of logic

  1. Go through the offers, best ROI first.
  2. Skip any bet that does not clear the bar.
  3. Take the rest, each up to its per-market cap.

It applies no portfolio rule at all, on purpose. It measures whether the mispricing exists after costs, not whether a sensible book can be built from it. The book it ends up with is lopsided: its average NO cost 36¢, and in the week of 09-21 EWY alone was 42% of the money.

4. direction_balanced

models/direction_balanced.py

Holds about as much "stock goes up" as "stock goes down", measured in dollars paid.

Slack = the larger of $150 and 10% of everything held.
  1. Edges. Each bet that clears the bar gets up to min(cap, room), where room = (dollars on the other lean) + slack − (dollars on its own lean, including this cycle's bets). With room under $1 it is refused.
  2. Hedges. If the book held is lopsided by more than the slack (a side resolved or a market touched), bets on the light lean that clear EV ≥ 0 come in, up to the size that levels the two leans and no further.

The other lean counts at what is held, not at this cycle's bets. Otherwise a bet the harness ends up not filling could make room for a bet on the opposite side.

What it does not balance is volatility. NO on both sides of a stock leans neither way and still loses on a big move in either direction. This model is the safeguard for direction only.

5. scenario_hedged

models/scenario_hedged.py

Keeps the whole book's risk small. It takes the same bets as edge_taker, best ROI first, but each only at the largest size that keeps the book inside two limits:

  • Spread: the book's standard deviation is at most 8% of what is held, with a $50 floor while the book is small.
  • Worst case: in no scenario does its expected PnL lose more than 10% of what is held, with a $50 floor.
  • A book that drifted outside a limit (a side resolved, a market touched, the clock ran down) may not drift further. While it is outside, bets that bring it back may come in at EV ≥ 0, each at the size that helps most.

The risk model, and why it looks like this

The first version of this model moved every stock the same number of sigmas in one direction and limited only the worst case. It made +81% in the week of 09-21 and −12% in another. Going through the bets showed three things it could not see:

  1. Volatility is the shared risk, not direction. Across every market priced from 09-16 to 10-16, touches came in at 0.72× the model's rate in the week of 09-21 and 1.59× in the week of 10-12. In a calm week every NO wins at once, above the price and below it. The old model counted NO above plus NO below as a hedge of each other.
  2. Bets on one ticker are one bet. EWY hit_up NO at 188, 192, 193 and 194 win or lose together. Counted that way, every week fell within ±1.6 sd of what the bets implied. Counted as independent coin flips, 09-21 looked like a +2.8 sd fluke.
  3. Cheap NO is a lopsided coin. A NO at 36¢ either loses everything or makes +180%. A worst-case limit does not see that spread.

So the model prices every bet in 49 scenarios: a volatility multiplier k (stocks move k times what options priced, log-sd 0.21) times a market drift μ (in implied sigmas, sd 0.39), each on 7 points out to ±3 sd and weighted by a normal density. Both sizes were fitted on these same five weeks, so treat them as provisional.

A bet's touch probability in a scenario comes from its own model probability q. For a random walk q = 2N(−a), where a is the barrier's distance in sigmas. In a scenario the distance becomes (a − μ)/k for an up barrier and (a + μ)/k for a down one. At k = 1, μ = 0 this gives q back.

The book's spread

The standard deviation adds two variances. Across: how much the book's expected PnL differs between scenarios, weighted by how likely each scenario is. This is the shared risk. Within: the coin flips left once the scenario is known. Per ticker × type × side, the spreads shares × √(p(1−p)) are added up and then squared, because bets in one cluster move together.

Before trusting any of this
  • scenario_hedged is steady because it is small. Its limit cuts exactly where the edge is (cheap NO clustered on a few tickers), and the bet that would let size back in, a YES that pays in a wild week, rarely clears EV ≥ 0. Looser limits trade steadiness for size: at 25%, $16.1k bet, +$2,204, and a +50% week.
  • Only part of the fills are confirmed, meaning the quote was still there when rechecked. edge_taker's confirmed-only PnL is about +$190 of the +$6,130.
  • Five weeks, one of them calm. The risk model's two factor sizes were fitted on the same weeks.
